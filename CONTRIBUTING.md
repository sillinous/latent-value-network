# Contributing to the Latent Value Network

Thank you for your interest in contributing to the Latent Value Network. This is an interdisciplinary initiative, and we value contributions from researchers, engineers, practitioners, ethicists, economists, and anyone who cares about building cooperative infrastructure for the future.

## How This Project Is Different

Most open-source projects are primarily code. The LVN is currently a **research and design project** that will eventually produce protocols and software. Right now, the most valuable contributions are:

- **Theoretical critiques** — Finding flaws in our reasoning
- **Research connections** — Linking relevant work we haven't considered
- **Domain expertise** — Bringing specialized knowledge to bear on specific layers
- **Practitioner perspective** — Grounding the architecture in real-world cooperative needs
- **Adversarial thinking** — Identifying how the system could be misused or captured

## Contribution Types

### 1. Discussion Contributions

The lowest-friction way to contribute. Open a GitHub Discussion to:

- **Challenge an assumption** in the white paper or architecture
- **Share related work** from your field that's relevant
- **Propose an alternative approach** to any architectural layer
- **Ask a question** that helps clarify the framework
- **Describe a use case** from your experience that illustrates the Latent Value Gap

### 2. Research Contributions

Submit a PR to the `research/` directory with:

- **Literature reviews** connecting the LVN to existing academic work
- **Quantitative analysis** of the Latent Value Gap in specific domains
- **Technical feasibility studies** of proposed architectural components
- **Comparative analysis** of related projects and approaches
- **Ethical analysis** of potential failure modes and unintended consequences

### 3. Specification Contributions

Submit a PR to the `specs/` directory with:

- **Formal specifications** for protocol components
- **Threat models** for privacy and security analysis
- **Interface definitions** between architectural layers
- **Governance framework** proposals

### 4. Working Group Participation

Join or form a working group focused on a specific aspect of the project. Current groups and how to join are listed in `community/working-groups/`.

## Contribution Guidelines

### Quality Standards

We value rigor over volume. A single well-reasoned critique is more valuable than a dozen surface-level suggestions.

- **Cite your sources.** If you're making a claim about what's technically possible or what research shows, provide references.
- **Be specific.** "This could be exploited" is less helpful than "Here's a specific attack vector against Layer 3 that exploits X."
- **Engage with the existing framework.** Read the white paper and relevant specifications before contributing to avoid duplication.
- **Acknowledge uncertainty.** It's fine to say "I think this might be a problem but I'm not sure" — that's more honest than false confidence.

### Tone and Culture

This project operates under a simple cultural principle: **be rigorous and be kind.** These are not in tension.

- Disagree with ideas, not people
- Assume good faith — especially across disciplinary boundaries
- Explain jargon from your field when communicating with the broader group
- Credit others' ideas generously
- If you don't know something, say so — that's how we learn

### Ethical Guardrails

Some contributions require extra care:

- **Exploitation analysis** — If you identify a way the system could be misused, describe it precisely enough that we can design against it, but don't provide a step-by-step exploitation guide.
- **Privacy-sensitive examples** — Use hypothetical scenarios rather than real people when illustrating cooperative matches.
- **Equity considerations** — Always ask: "Who might this leave out?" and "Could this harm the most vulnerable?"

## Pull Request Process

1. **Fork the repository** and create a feature branch
2. **Write your contribution** following the relevant template in `community/templates/`
3. **Submit a PR** with a clear description of what you're contributing and why
4. **Engage with review** — expect questions and suggestions from other contributors
5. **Iterate** — the best contributions often go through several rounds of refinement

## Recognition

All contributors are recognized in `CONTRIBUTORS.md`. We use an expansive definition of contribution — rigorous critique, domain expertise, practitioner insight, and community building are all valued alongside traditional research and engineering contributions.

## Code of Conduct

We follow a straightforward code of conduct: treat everyone with respect, engage in good faith, and prioritize the mission of building cooperative infrastructure that serves all people. See `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md` for details.

## Getting Started

1. **Read the white paper** — `docs/white-paper/LVN_White_Paper_v0.1.docx`
2. **Browse open Discussions** — see what conversations are already happening
3. **Introduce yourself** — open a Discussion in the "Introductions" category sharing your background and what drew you to this project
4. **Pick something that interests you** — and dig in
