# Pilot Community Assessment Criteria

**How We Select Communities for LVN Pilot Deployment**

---

## Required Criteria

A pilot community MUST have:

- [ ] **Geographic coherence** — participants can physically meet (within ~30 mile radius)
- [ ] **Institutional anchor** — a library, community center, church, or similar institution willing to serve as trust anchor and host onboarding sessions
- [ ] **Diverse skill mix** — not a homogeneous professional community (variety of practical, professional, relational skills)
- [ ] **50-200 potential participants** — large enough for meaningful matching, small enough to monitor
- [ ] **Community champion(s)** — at least 1 local leader willing to facilitate onboarding and support
- [ ] **Informed consent** — all participants understand what data is processed, how, and can withdraw at any time
- [ ] **Ethics & Equity sign-off** — passes the equity pre-assessment before deployment

## Preferred Criteria

Higher priority given to communities that:

- [ ] Have visible unmet cooperative potential (the latent value gap is observable)
- [ ] Are in rural or small-town settings (where the gap is typically largest)
- [ ] Have basic technology access (smartphones, internet — doesn't need to be universal)
- [ ] Are not already well-served by existing platforms (timebanks, mutual aid apps, etc.)
- [ ] Include participants across age ranges, income levels, and backgrounds
- [ ] Have local media willing to document the pilot (for evidence and outreach)

## Equity Pre-Assessment

Before deploying, the Ethics & Equity group evaluates:

1. **Who is likely to participate?** If participation skews toward already-connected residents, the pilot reinforces existing inequity.
2. **Who is likely to be excluded?** Technology barriers, language barriers, trust barriers — how are these addressed?
3. **Who benefits most?** If benefits accrue primarily to high-skill individuals, the matching parameters need adjustment.
4. **What happens when the pilot ends?** Is there a plan for continuity or graceful shutdown?
5. **What could go wrong?** Specific failure scenarios for this community.

## Evaluation Metrics

During the pilot, we measure:

| Metric | Target | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| Match rate | >60% of participants receive at least 1 match | System utility |
| Cross-boundary rate | >30% of matches cross social clusters | Core thesis validation |
| Acceptance rate | >40% of matches proceed past disclosure L1 | Handshake design validation |
| Cooperative outcome | >20% of accepted matches result in action | Real-world value |
| Equity distribution | Match rate variance <20% across demographics | Equity |
| Satisfaction | >70% "would recommend" | User experience |
| Privacy incidents | 0 | Non-negotiable |

## Primary Target: Newton, Illinois

Newton (pop. ~2,800) is the initial target for pilot assessment due to:
- Small-town setting with visible cooperative potential
- Existing community institutions (library, churches, community organizations)
- Rural Illinois context — limited access to specialized services creates clear needs
- Founder proximity enabling intensive facilitation support

Assessment materials for Newton are in `newton-il/`.
