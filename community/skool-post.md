# The Latent Value Network — Building Cooperative Infrastructure for the Age of AI

**Looking for collaborators on an open initiative that sits squarely at the intersection of what this community thinks about every day.**

---

## The Problem

Most value that *could* be exchanged between people never is — not because people are unwilling, but because neither party knows the other exists.

A retired mechanic lives three miles from a single parent whose car problem she could diagnose in ten minutes. A small business owner struggles with a marketing challenge that a college student two blocks away has exactly the skills to address. A farmer sits on surplus equipment that would transform a neighbor’s operation.

This isn’t a market failure in the traditional sense. It’s an **information failure**. The knowledge required to connect complementary needs and capacities is distributed, private, contextual, and dynamic. No existing system — marketplaces, job boards, social networks — captures more than a fraction of it, because all of them require you to *already know what you need* and *actively search for it*.

We call this the **Latent Value Gap**.

## The Opportunity

Here’s what changes with AI: as assistants become embedded in daily life — managing communications, helping with decisions, navigating problems — they naturally develop rich contextual models of individual situations. Not through surveillance, but as the *byproduct of being useful*.

This ambient context is exactly the information needed to identify latent complementarities between people. The question is whether it stays siloed inside individual AI interactions, or becomes the basis for **cooperative infrastructure**.

## What We’re Building

The **Latent Value Network (LVN)** is a framework — protocol-layer, not platform — for AI-mediated human cooperation at scale. Five architectural layers:

1. **Personal Context Engine** — Your AI builds a private, sovereign model of your situation, capabilities, and needs. It never leaves your control.
2. **Latent Capacity Discovery** — AI surfaces capabilities you didn’t know you had. You’ve fixed three plumbing issues this year? That’s latent expertise. You have a truck and free Tuesday mornings? That’s latent logistics capacity.
3. **Signal Exchange Protocol** — Privacy-preserving matching using ZK proofs, federated learning, and progressive disclosure. Agents discover complementary fits without exposing private context.
4. **Trust & Reputation Fabric** — Distributed, contextual, relational trust. No centralized reputation scores. Trust flows through chains, not platforms.
5. **Governance Layer** — Ostrom-informed commons governance. Anti-capture mechanisms at the protocol level, not the policy level.

## The Principles (Non-Negotiable)

- **Sovereignty First** — You own your context. No central entity stores or profits from it.
- **Agency Preserved** — The system surfaces possibilities. Humans decide. Always invitation, never assignment.
- **Exploitation Prevented** — Structural resistance to capture — by corporations, governments, or any extraction mechanism. Protocol-level, not promises.
- **Equity by Design** — The people who most need cooperative connections are often the least equipped to adopt new tech. The architecture must account for this from day one.
- **Pluralism of Value** — Not everything is a market transaction. The system must accommodate gifts, reciprocity, mentorship, and mutual aid without reducing everything to a price.
- **Harm Inversion** — If a capability can’t be made safe by design, it doesn’t get built.

## Why This Community

David’s work on post-labor economics, cognitive architectures, and the NLCA/ACE framework has been a significant influence on how I’ve been thinking about this. The LVN sits at the exact convergence point of questions this community wrestles with:

- What does cooperative infrastructure look like when AI handles increasingly more labor?
- How do we design systems that distribute value rather than concentrate it?
- How do we build AI coordination that preserves human agency and sovereignty?
- What does Hayek’s knowledge problem look like when AI agents can actually *solve* parts of it?

This isn’t a startup pitch. There’s no token, no equity raise, no platform play. It’s an open collaborative initiative released under Creative Commons, and it needs people who think seriously about these problems.

## What Exists So Far

- **A foundational white paper** (~5,000 words) grounding the problem in information economics, network theory (Granovetter, Burt), privacy-preserving computation, and commons governance (Ostrom)
- **A live collaborative suite** — a real-time Firebase-backed web app where contributors can join working groups, post discussions, submit proposals, and use AI-assisted research tools to develop ideas together
- **A conceptual architecture** mapping to emerging AI protocols (MCP, A2A, ACP, UCP)
- **A GitHub repository** organizing the project materials

## What I’m Looking For

I’m looking for people who want to **think rigorously** about this and **contribute to the architecture** — not just react to it. Specifically:

- **AI/ML folks** — context modeling, federated systems, agent coordination
- **Cryptographers** — privacy-preserving matching, ZK applications
- **Economists** — mechanism design, incentive structures, commons economics
- **Network scientists** — topology, propagation, emergence
- **Ethicists** — consent frameworks, power dynamics, exploitation prevention
- **Systems architects** — protocol design, distributed systems, interoperability
- **Domain practitioners** — mutual aid organizers, community workers, anyone who witnesses the Latent Value Gap in practice

But honestly? If you’ve spent time in this community thinking about post-labor futures and cooperative systems, you probably have relevant perspective regardless of your formal background. That’s kind of the whole point.

## How to Get Involved

Drop a comment or DM me. I’ll share access to the collaborative suite and the full white paper. The project is designed for asynchronous, distributed collaboration — contribute what you can, when you can.

The goal at this stage isn’t code. It’s **rigorous conceptual development** — stress-testing the architecture, finding the flaws, proposing alternatives, and building the theoretical foundation that makes eventual implementation worth doing.
