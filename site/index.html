The Invisible Crisis

The Latent Value Gap

A retired engineer who could diagnose your drainage problem in ten minutes lives three miles away. A college student builds websites for fun while the food bank next door drowns in spreadsheets. Neither knows the other exists. No marketplace, social network, or job board can find these matches — because neither party would think to search.

This connection died in silence.

Maria could save James thousands. James could build what Maria's library needs. Three miles apart. Invisible to each other. This happens millions of times, every day, everywhere.

The Architecture

Five Layers of Cooperative Infrastructure

The LVN is a protocol, not a platform. It enables AI agents that already understand your situation to discover cooperative matches — without sharing your private data.

1

Personal Context Engine

Your AI assistant already understands your skills, challenges, and resources through normal conversation. This context stays on your device — sovereign and private.

2

Latent Capacity Discovery

AI surfaces capabilities you don't recognize in yourself. Resolved three insurance disputes? That's latent negotiation expertise. Explain medical info to your family? That's health communication capacity.

3

Signal Exchange Protocol

Your agent emits abstract, non-identifying signals — like a lock that only responds to specific key shapes. Complementary agents detect matches without either party exposing private context.

4

Trust & Reputation Fabric

Distributed trust flows through relational chains — contextual, privacy-preserving, and bootstrappable through small cooperative acts. No centralized trust score.

5

Governance Layer

Following Ostrom's principles for commons governance. Anti-capture mechanisms built into the protocol. Community-driven evolution with equity monitoring.

Your Path In

We need different minds for different problems

The LVN is deliberately multidisciplinary. Here's where your expertise fits.

For Researchers & Academics

The LVN draws on network science, information economics, mechanism design, and trust research — but several core technical problems remain unsolved. The protocol specification identifies six formal open questions, each of which could be a research contribution. We're particularly looking for people working on privacy-preserving computation, social network analysis, and commons governance.

For Engineers & Developers

The signal exchange protocol needs implementation. We need people who understand MCP, A2A, distributed systems, and cryptographic primitives. The protocol spec defines data structures, state machines, and integration points — but we haven't written a line of implementation code yet. The architecture is designed to build on existing AI coordination standards.

For Community Practitioners & Organizers

You know what cooperation actually looks like on the ground — the mutual aid networks, the informal help that keeps communities functioning. The LVN needs your reality check. Does this architecture match how cooperation actually works? What are we missing? We're designing pilot programs and need practitioners who can tell us when we're being naive.

For Economists & Ethicists

The hardest problems in the LVN aren't technical — they're about incentives, governance, and values. How do you design a value exchange system that accommodates gifts and markets simultaneously? How do you govern a digital commons without recreating the platform capture problem? How do you ensure the system serves the least-connected, not just the most?

The Hard Questions

10 Provocations That Need Your Mind

These are genuine debates where reasonable people disagree. Pick the one that annoys you most — where you think we're wrong — and tell us why.

01
Should the embedding model be centralized or federated?
Who controls what "complementarity" means controls who gets matched with whom.
02
Is privacy-preserving semantic matching actually possible?
ZK proofs work for exact attributes. Can they handle fuzzy, semantic complementarity?
03
Does ambient matching inevitably become surveillance?
A system that understands your needs also understands your vulnerabilities.
04
How do you prevent the network from only serving the already-connected?
Technology adoption follows power laws. The early adopters aren't the people who need this most.
05
Should matches include a price?
Does adding value signals make exchanges more equitable — or commodify cooperation?
